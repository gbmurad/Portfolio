Rad Shields privacy policy

Last updated: November 2026

Rad Shields does not collect, store on a server, share or sell any personal information.

What stays in your browser. Your settings, the sites you turned the shield off for, the sites and page elements you chose to block, a running count of blocked requests, and the filter lists with the rules built from them. All of this lives in Chrome's local extension storage on your own computer. Removing the extension removes it.

What the extension reads. To block and hide ads it works on the pages you visit and the requests they make. That happens inside your browser. None of it is sent anywhere, and the extension has no server, account or analytics.

What it downloads. Public filter lists, as plain text, from easylist.to, ublockorigin.github.io, pgl.yoyo.org and secure.fanboy.co.nz, on first start and then once a day. These requests carry no information about you beyond what any browser request carries, such as your IP address, which those list hosts can see.

Remote code. None. The lists are data. All code is in the extension package.

Children. The extension does not collect data from anyone, including children.

Changes. If this ever changes, the new policy will be published with the update before it takes effect.

Contact. The developer of Rad Shields, through the Chrome Web Store listing.
